# Citations vs Recommendations

## Visibility ladder

1. **Retrieved** — page is in the candidate set for a query / fan-out.
2. **Cited** — AI uses your page as a source (link or attribution).
3. **Mentioned** — brand/product named in the answer without necessarily citing you.
4. **Recommended** — you land on the buyer’s shortlist (“use X”, “best for Y”).

Citation ≠ recommendation. Citation means you were useful to consult. Recommendation comes mostly from **web-wide consensus** (reviews, forums, analysts, press), not from self-promotional pages alone.

## Stage-dependent strategy

| Stage | Goal | Content emphasis |
|-------|------|------------------|
| Awareness | Retrieved + cited | Definitions, guides, original data |
| Consideration | Mentioned + compared fairly | Balanced comparisons, docs, pricing clarity |
| Decision | Recommended | Third-party proof, reviews, case studies, analyst mentions |

## What earns recommendations

- Consistent positive mentions on G2/Capterra/TrustRadius, Reddit, niche forums.
- Press / roundups / “best of” written by **independent** publishers.
- Clear product–fit signals agents can parse (pricing, limits, integrations).
- Avoid only publishing “we’re the best [category]” listicles from your own domain.

## Self-promotional listicle risk

Emerging brands that publish self-serving “best tools” pages may get **cited** in answers that still **recommend competitors**. Optimize for extractable authority on topics you truly own; earn recommendations via third-party consensus.

## Attribution blind spot

Traffic from AI answers is often undercounted (zero-click, copied answers, odd referrers). Track:

- Brand citation / share of AI voice (Otterly, Peec, ZipTie, LLMrefs).
- Manual prompt panels for top queries.
- Direct/branded search lift as a proxy.

Measure both **citation rate** and **recommendation framing** (“recommended”, “consider”, “avoid”).
